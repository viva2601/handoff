Q: What are the standard check-in and check-out times?
A: Check-in is usually from 2 PM and check-out is by 11 AM. Times can vary by hotel.

Q: Are pets allowed?
A: Pet policies vary by hotel. Pet-friendly hotels may charge a cleaning fee and limit pet size.

Q: Is breakfast included?
A: Breakfast is included only in rates marked 'breakfast included'. Otherwise it can be added at the hotel.

Q: Is Wi-Fi free?
A: Most hotels offer free Wi-Fi in rooms and public areas. Some premium properties charge for high-speed access.

Q: What is the hotel cancellation policy?
A: Free cancellation is available on flexible rates up to 24 to 48 hours before check-in, depending on the hotel. Non-refundable and discounted rates cannot be cancelled or changed without losing the full amount. Late cancellations and no-shows are usually charged the first night. The cancellation terms are shown on the rate before you book.

Q: Can I book a room for someone else?
A: Yes, you can book for another guest. The booking should be made in the name of the person staying, or they should carry a confirmation with your details. The hotel may ask the guest for ID at check-in. If the card used to pay is not the guest's, the hotel may request a copy of the card and an authorisation letter.

Q: Can I request early check-in or late check-out?
A: Early check-in and late check-out are subject to availability and are not guaranteed. Contact the hotel a day or two ahead to request them. Some hotels offer them free, while others charge a half-day or hourly fee. Luggage can usually be stored at the front desk if your room is not ready.

Q: How many guests can stay in one room?
A: Each room type has a maximum occupancy, commonly two adults for standard rooms and up to four for family rooms or suites. Children under a certain age, often 5 or 6, may stay free in existing bedding. Extra beds are available on request for a fee, subject to room size and fire-safety limits.

Q: What ID and payment details are needed at check-in?
A: All adult guests must show a valid government-issued photo ID at check-in, such as a passport or national ID card. International guests are usually required to present a passport and visa, since hotels must register foreign guests with local authorities. A credit or debit card is typically requested to cover incidental charges, and the hotel may place a temporary hold on the card as a security deposit. This hold is released after check-out, though it can take several working days to appear back in your account. Some hotels accept cash deposits instead. If you prepaid through a third party, bring the confirmation and the card used for payment in case the hotel asks for verification.

Q: What does travel or hotel insurance cover?
A: Travel insurance can cover non-refundable hotel costs if you have to cancel for a reason listed in the policy, such as illness, a family emergency, or a flight cancellation. It may also cover lost belongings, medical expenses and interruption of your trip. It generally does not cover cancellations for reasons you simply change your mind about, pre-existing conditions that were not declared, or events specifically excluded in the policy. Insurance is bought separately from the hotel booking, and claims need supporting documents such as the booking confirmation, receipts and a doctor's note. Always read the policy exclusions before purchasing.

Q: What facilities and accessibility options do hotels provide?
A: Common facilities include a front desk open around the clock, a restaurant or breakfast area, a fitness centre, and in many cases a pool, spa or business centre. Accessible rooms are available at many hotels, with features such as step-free entry, wider doorways, roll-in showers and grab rails. These rooms are limited, so they should be requested in advance and reconfirmed with the hotel before arrival. Parking, airport shuttles and laundry services are often available, sometimes for an extra charge.

Q: What if my room is not as described or I have a complaint?
A: Report the problem to the front desk as soon as you notice it, so the hotel has the chance to fix it or move you to another room during your stay. Take photos as evidence and note the time and the staff member you spoke with. If the issue is not resolved, ask to speak to the duty manager and request a written record of your complaint. After checkout, you can raise the matter with the booking platform or the hotel's guest relations team, attaching your evidence, and ask for a refund or partial credit where appropriate.
